# MBX

MBX shapes dynamics within chosen frequency ranges on individual tracks, buses, and mixes. It uses Toybox and ships first as a native macOS VST3.

Start with three bands spanning the spectrum. Split any band where a separate dynamics treatment is useful, adjust the shared boundaries, and set each band’s gain curve and timings. A band may be bypassed while it keeps its frequency range. Monitor the result in context or audition that band for diagnosis. Automate the working controls in the DAW and restore them when reopening a project.

Start with a full-width spectrum for locating energy and creating, placing, adjusting, and soloing bands. Open the band list for a compact view of all band levels. Show rotary controls above the frequency ruler when a band is selected. Slide the controls horizontally with the band and keep them inside the spectrum width. Curve the range limits with the crossover slopes so settings and live gain movement are legible in context. Open the detector controls to choose a trigger source and drag the detector limits on the spectrum. Show output peaks on a separate vertical dBFS scale beside the spectrum. Keep phase, lookahead, mix, output, bypass, and analyzer controls in the footer. Let users resize the host window on either axis or enter fullscreen on the display containing the editor. Keep preset browsing and A/B comparison visible while editing. Loading a preset affects the active comparison side. Preserve the alternate settings with the project. Use Tab to move between controls, Enter or Space for discrete controls, and arrows to adjust values.

The first build uses a dark black-and-white theme with neutral grays, red accents, and compact value controls. The initial mastering and mixing criteria are unity gain at neutral settings, predictable gain limits, quiet automation, and isolation from adjacent frequency ranges. Minimum and linear phase are selectable. Enable the global lookahead engine before assigning a per-band preview.
